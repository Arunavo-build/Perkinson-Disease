Section S1. Standard Acoustic Signal Processing and B-Spline Equations

This section provides the standard mathematical definitions for the acoustic feature families and B-spline basis functions referenced in Section III of the main text.

1) Fundamental Frequency Perturbation (Local and RAP Jitter): Given K consecutive glottal pitch periods T1, T2, …, TK extracted via autocorrelation peak picking, cycle-to-cycle local percentage jitter and three-point relative average perturbation (RAP) are defined as:

Jitterloc(%) = [ (1 / (K − 1)) Σi=1K−1 |Ti − Ti+1| ] / [ (1 / K) Σi=1K Ti ] × 100,   RAP = [ (1 / (K − 2)) Σi=2K−1 |Ti − (Ti−1 + Ti + Ti+1)/3| ] / [ (1 / K) Σi=1K Ti ]. (S1)

2) Amplitude Perturbation (Local and APQ Shimmer): Given peak-to-peak glottal cycle amplitudes A1, A2, …, AK, local shimmer in decibels (dB) and the q-point amplitude perturbation quotient (APQq, q ∈ {3, 5, 11}) are:

ShimmerdB = (1 / (K − 1)) Σi=1K−1 |20 log10(Ai+1 / Ai)|,   APQq = [ (1 / (K − q + 1)) Σi=1K−q+1 |Ai − (1/q)Σm=0q−1 Ai+m| ] / [ (1 / K) Σi=1K Ai ]. (S2)

3) Harmonics-to-Noise Ratio (HNR) and Pitch Period Entropy (PPE): For an acoustic frame s(t) with fundamental period T0, HNR quantifies harmonic periodicity relative to turbulent glottal noise, while PPE measures the Shannon entropy of the pitch-period residual distribution pr on a semitone scale:

HNR (dB) = 10 log10 [ R(T0) / (R(0) − R(T0)) ],   PPE = −Σr pr log2(pr). (S3)

4) Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCCs) and Dynamic Velocity/Acceleration: Using a B-channel triangular Mel filterbank Hb(k) applied to the short-time Fourier power spectrum |S(k)|2, static cepstral coefficients cn and their first-order (Δcn, velocity) and second-order (Δ2cn, acceleration) temporal derivatives over window W = 2 are computed as:

cn = Σb=1B log(Σk |S(k)|2 Hb(k)) cos[πn(b − 0.5)/B],   Δcn(t) = [ Στ=1W τ(cn(t+τ) − cn(t−τ)) ] / [ 2 Στ=1W τ2 ]. (S4)

5) Tunable Q-Factor Wavelet Transform (TQWT): TQWT decomposes the voice signal into J = 36 sub-bands parameterized by quality factor Q, redundancy r, and low/high-pass scaling factors α = 1 − 2/[(Q + 1)r], β = 2/(Q + 1). The sub-band log-energy entropy of level j with wavelet coefficients wj,n is:

Elog, j = Σn=1Nj log(wj,n2 + ε),   TKEO(wj,n) = wj,n2 − wj,n−1 wj,n+1. (S5)

6) Cox–de Boor Cubic B-Spline Recursion in KAN: On a uniform knot sequence {t0, t1, …, tG+2k} over [−2.5, 2.5], the degree-k (k = 3) B-spline basis functions Bm,k(z) used in Eq. (2) of the main manuscript are evaluated recursively from piecewise-constant indicators:

Bm,0(z) = I(tm ≤ z < tm+1),   Bm,k(z) = [(z − tm)/(tm+k − tm)] Bm,k−1(z) + [(tm+k+1 − z)/(tm+k+1 − tm+1)] Bm+1,k−1(z). (S6)

Table S3 (Static vs. Dynamic MFCC Correspondence and Polarity Analysis): Beyond the 27 shared perturbation, formant, and energy features in Table S2, both TR and BN extract 13 static Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (mean_MFCC_0th_coef–mean_MFCC_12th_coef in TR vs. MFCC_1–MFCC_13 in BN) and their first-order temporal derivatives (mean_1st_delta–mean_12th_delta in TR vs. Delta_MFCC_1–Delta_MFCC_12 in BN). As shown in Section IV-D and Fig. 5(c) of the main manuscript, static spectral envelope coefficients such as MFCC_2 preserve consistent positive effect directionality across languages (TR Cohen's d = +0.95; BN Cohen's d = +0.96), whereas first-order cepstral velocities (Delta_MFCC_2) invert sign between sustained vowel phonation (d = −0.24 in TR) and spontaneous conversation (d = +0.96 in BN) due to stationary vocal tract rigidity versus dynamic articulatory transition undershoot.
